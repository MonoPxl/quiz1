FLAVOURS

Put Ambon on the table.

Food is another way into a place. Here, sago, fish, coconut, spices, kenari, and local citrus build a flavor profile that feels unmistakably Mollucan.

01 · STAPLE

Papeda & Yellow Fish Soup

A sago-based staple commonly paired with fresh fish and a fragrant yellow broth. Its mild taste and distinctive texture make the soup and its sides do the talking.


The most iconic part of eating Papeda isn't just the flavor—it's the highly interactive, almost theatrical way it's served and eaten. Because papeda is incredibly sticky and elastic, you can't just scoop it out with a spoon. Instead, you use a pair of wooden fork-like bamboo sticks called gata-gata. You dip the sticks into the communal pot, pinch a portion of the sticky mass, and twirl them rapidly in opposite directions, like winding up a ball of yarn, to sever the thick, stretchy strands. Once you've successfully rolled it up, you quickly drop it into your personal bowl of yellow soup. And the golden rule once it's in your bowl? Don't chew. Papeda is meant to be slurped directly from the bowl and swallowed whole along with the savory broth. If you try to chew it, it will simply bounce around and stick to your teeth!
